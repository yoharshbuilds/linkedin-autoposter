# How Rahul Mathur researches before he writes

He does not summarize the news. He goes to the primary numbers, finds a nuance the
coverage missed, and shows the math. Replicate this process before writing any post.

## 1. Go to primary sources (in this order of trust)
- RHP / DRHP filings (IPO prospectus): cap tables, share counts, cost basis, FMV, fund-wise holdings.
- MCA / ROC filings (annual financials): revenue, PAT, expenses, specific P&L line items.
- Company quarterly / FY filings and investor letters: order volumes, take rates, segment costs.
- TRACXN / Crunchbase: round history, entry valuations, stake percentages, dates.
- Official company press releases and the actual deal terms (not the headline).
- Reputable trade press (Inc42, Entrackr, Moneycontrol) only to corroborate, not as the number.

## 2. Pull the specific line items he needs
Depending on the story, find the concrete inputs:
- VC returns: amount invested, entry valuation, stake %, share count, cost basis, current/exit
  valuation or FMV, round dates, fund size, prior realized returns, which fund/vehicle holds it.
- Financials: revenue (from operations), PAT, growth %, the relevant cost line, order/unit volume.
- M&A / IPO: deal size, valuation, what % is being bought, secondary vs primary.

## 3. Compute the derived metric (his signature) and SHOW the formula
- MOIC = current value (or FMV) / cost basis.
- Gross multiple, XIRR / IRR where dates are known.
- Implied unit economics: cost line / units (e.g. support cost per order, CAC, contribution).
- % change YoY, per-order or per-user normalization.
- Always state the formula and the inputs, e.g. "Rs 112.47 cr FMV / Rs 18.02 cr cost = 6.24x".
- Distinguish nuances he loves: which fund/vehicle, continuity vehicles, de-duplicated vs reported,
  gross vs net of fees, realized vs unrealized.

## 4. Hard discipline (non-negotiable)
- Use ONLY numbers you actually found in a source. Never invent or guess a figure.
- If a needed number cannot be found, mark it UNKNOWN and do NOT compute a metric that depends on it.
- Keep each fact tied to where it came from (filing / source name).
- Prefer the most recent filing. Note the period (FY25, Q4 FY26) for every figure.
- A post with one rock-solid computed number beats a post with three shaky ones.
